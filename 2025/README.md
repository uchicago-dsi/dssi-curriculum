# Data Science for Social Impact (DSSI) — Summer 2025 Curriculum

## Overview

This directory contains the instructional materials used during the  **Summer 2025 Data Science for Social Impact (DSSI) program** . Materials are organized to reflect  **what was actually taught**, in the order it was taught.

The 2025 program is explicitly designed as a  **two-week curriculum** . The  **first instructional week is identical in structure, content, and sequencing to the Summer 2024 curriculum** , ensuring continuity across cohorts and a shared foundational baseline. The  **second week extends this foundation** , with deeper emphasis on **machine learning and natural language processing (NLP)**.

This README is written so that someone **not involved in the 2025 program** can understand the instructional progression and organization.

---

## Program Scope (2025)

* **Duration:** Two instructional weeks (Monday–Friday, Week 1 and Week 2)
* **Primary focus:** Core data science foundations followed by applied machine learning and NLP
* **Programming language:** Python
* **Key tools:** pandas, matplotlib/seaborn, scikit-learn, PyTorch, NLTK, SpaCy
* **Project emphasis:** Instructional preparation followed by research-oriented application

### Relationship to 2024

* **Week 1 (Foundations):** Identical to Summer 2024 content
* **Week 2 (Extensions):** New material focused on NLP and modern ML methods

Only materials that were **actively used during instruction** are included in this directory.

---

## Instructional Structure

The instructional period consists of two weeks:

* **Week 1:** Core data science foundations (mirrors 2024)
* **Week 2:** Machine learning and natural language processing

Lecture numbering corresponds to the  **day of instruction** .

---

## Week 1 — Foundations (Identical to 2024)

Week 1 is a direct continuation of the Summer 2024 curriculum and is included to ensure a common baseline for all fellows.

### Weekly Schedule (Week 1)

| Day       | Repository Label | Theme                                     |
| --------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Monday    | Lecture 1        | Pandas, DataFrames, and Grouping          |
| Tuesday   | Lecture 2        | Visualization and Debugging               |
| Wednesday | Lecture 3        | Probability, Distributions, and Functions |
| Thursday  | Lecture 4        | Classification and Machine Learning       |
| Friday    | Lecture 5        | Data Ethics and Text Processing           |

### Instructional Progression (Week 1)

#### Day 1 (Monday): Pandas, DataFrames, and Grouping

* Program orientation
* Review of Python and Jupyter notebook fundamentals
* Tabular data and DataFrames
* Groupby operations, pivot tables, and aggregation
* Applied examples using real datasets

---

#### Day 2 (Tuesday): Visualization and Exploratory Analysis

* Visualization principles
* Building and interpreting plots
* Exploratory data analysis workflows
* Debugging strategies and common errors

---

#### Day 3 (Wednesday): Probability, Distributions, and Functions

* Probability concepts and distributions
* Empirical vs. theoretical reasoning
* Documentation and reproducibility
* User-defined functions
* Lab-based reinforcement

---

#### Day 4 (Thursday): Classification and Machine Learning

* Classification fundamentals
* Binary and multi-class classification
* Model intuition and evaluation
* Introductory machine learning concepts

---

#### Day 5 (Friday): Data Ethics and Text Processing

* Ethical considerations in data science
* Introduction to text and unstructured data
* Regular expressions (regex)
* Practical text-cleaning and pattern-matching workflows
* Week 1 synthesis

---

## Week 2 — Machine Learning and Natural Language Processing

Week 2 builds on the shared foundation established in Week 1 and introduces students to **modern machine learning and NLP techniques** that will be used during the research phase of the fellowship.

### Instructional Themes (Week 2)

* Machine learning models for classification
* Text representation and feature extraction
* Word embeddings and language models
* Neural network architectures for language
* Applied NLP tasks

Topics covered include:

* Supervised learning for text classification
* Tokenization and linguistic preprocessing
* Word embeddings
* Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and LSTMs
* Attention mechanisms
* Transformer-based models (e.g., BERT)

Students implement models using **Python** and Python-based libraries such as  **PyTorch** ,  **NLTK** , and  **SpaCy** , applying them to tasks including:

* Text classification
* Token classification
* Text generation

## Pedagogical Intent

The 2025 curriculum is designed to:

* Establish a strong, shared foundation in data science
* Ensure continuity across program years
* Gradually transition students from foundational tools to modern ML and NLP
* Prepare fellows for independent research during the remainder of the program

This structure reflects a deliberate progression from **core data skills** to  **research-ready computational methods** .
